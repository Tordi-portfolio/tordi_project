📘 About Porosity

Porosity (φ) is the fraction of void space in a rock that can store fluids such as oil, gas, or water. It is a key reservoir quality indicator in petrophysics.

📐 Formula (Density Log Method)

φ = (ρma − ρb) / (ρma − ρf)

📊 Units

  • ρma (Matrix density): g/cm³
  • ρb (Bulk density): g/cm³
  • ρf (Fluid density): g/cm³
  • Porosity is dimensionless (or fraction / %)

⚠️ How to Use

Enter all density values in g/cm³. If you are using percentage porosity, divide the final result by 100.

Bulk, Matrix and Fluid Density Relationship

Understanding how density variations affect porosity, pore spaces and wellbore behavior in reservoir formations.

Porosity Formula (Density Method)

ϕ = (ρma - ρb) / (ρma - ρf)

Bulk Density Increases

When bulk density increases, the rock becomes more compact and contains less pore space.

Effects on Pore Space

  • Pore spaces decrease
  • Porosity reduces
  • Reservoir becomes tighter
  • Less hydrocarbon storage

Effects on Wellbore

  • Reduced fluid flow
  • Lower production rate
  • Permeability decreases
  • Formation becomes harder

Bulk Density Decreases

Lower bulk density usually indicates higher pore volume and better reservoir quality.

Effects on Pore Space

  • Pore spaces increase
  • Higher porosity
  • Better hydrocarbon accumulation
  • Improved reservoir quality

Effects on Wellbore

  • Improved oil and gas flow
  • Higher permeability
  • Better production potential
  • Easier fluid movement

Matrix Density Changes

Matrix density represents the density of solid rock grains without pore spaces.

If Matrix Density Increases

  • Rock becomes denser
  • Formation may contain carbonates
  • Drilling may become harder
  • Bit wear increases

If Matrix Density Decreases

  • Softer formations appear
  • Possible shale presence
  • Wellbore instability risk
  • Sand production may occur

Fluid Density Changes

Fluid density affects the fluid occupying the pore spaces inside the reservoir.

If Fluid Density Increases

  • Water saturation increases
  • Heavy fluids enter pores
  • Hydrostatic pressure increases
  • Blowout risk reduces

If Fluid Density Decreases

  • Gas zones may be present
  • Lower pressure support
  • Kick risk increases
  • Possible hydrocarbon indication

Bulk Density ↓ Pore space increases Better reservoir quality Improved production

Porosity Formula Library

Important undergraduate porosity formulas used in petrophysics, reservoir engineering and well log analysis.

1. Basic Porosity Formula

ϕ = Vp / Vb

This is the fundamental porosity equation showing the ratio of pore volume to bulk volume.

  • ϕ = Porosity
  • Vp = Pore Volume
  • Vb = Bulk Volume

2. Percentage Porosity Formula

ϕ(%) = (Vp / Vb) × 100

Used to express porosity in percentage form.

3. Density Porosity Formula

ϕ = (ρma − ρb) / (ρma − ρf)

The most common density log equation used in petrophysical analysis.

  • ρma = Matrix Density
  • ρb = Bulk Density
  • ρf = Fluid Density

4. Sonic Porosity Formula

ϕ = (Δtlog − Δtma) / (Δtf − Δtma)

Used in sonic logging to estimate formation porosity.

  • Δtlog = Formation Transit Time
  • Δtma = Matrix Transit Time
  • Δtf = Fluid Transit Time

5. Effective Porosity Formula

ϕe = Vconnected pores / Vb

Effective porosity includes only connected pore spaces capable of transmitting fluids.

6. Total Porosity Formula

ϕt = Vtotal pores / Vb

Total porosity includes all connected and isolated pores.

7. Secondary Porosity Formula

ϕs = ϕt − ϕp

Secondary porosity develops due to fractures, dissolution and diagenesis.

8. Average Porosity Formula

ϕavg = Σ(ϕi hi) / Σhi

Used for calculating average porosity across multiple reservoir layers.

9. Dual Porosity Formula

ϕt = ϕm + ϕf

Used in fractured carbonate reservoirs where matrix and fracture porosity coexist.

10. Porosity Compaction Formula

ϕ = ϕ₀e⁻ᶜᶻ

Shows how porosity decreases with increasing depth due to compaction.

  • ϕ₀ = Initial Porosity
  • c = Compaction Constant
  • z = Depth
