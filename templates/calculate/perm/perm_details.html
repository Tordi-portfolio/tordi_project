About Permeability

Permeability (k) measures the ability of a rock to transmit fluids through its pore network. It is a key parameter in reservoir productivity analysis.

Empirical Formula

k = C × φ⁴ / Sw²

Darcy Core Flood Formula

k = (Q × μ × L) / (A × ΔP)

Units

  • k: Darcy or milliDarcy (mD)
  • Q: cm³/s
  • μ (viscosity): cP
  • L: cm
  • A: cm²
  • ΔP: atm or bar (be consistent)

How to Use

Ensure consistent units before calculation. Core flood method is used for lab data, while empirical method is used for log-based estimation.

Empirical vs Darcy Method (Key Difference)

Empirical Method (Log-Based Estimation)

The empirical method estimates permeability using rock properties such as porosity and water saturation. It is derived from statistical relationships rather than direct physical flow experiments.

Empirical Equation:

k = C × φ⁴ / Sw²

Relationship Meaning

  • Porosity (φ): Directly proportional to permeability
  • Water saturation (Sw): Inversely proportional
  • Constant C: Represents lithology and rock type calibration factor

What happens when values change?

  • φ increases → permeability increases sharply (exponential effect)
  • Sw increases → permeability decreases (more water blocks flow paths)
  • C increases → indicates more permeable lithology

Darcy Core Flood Method (Physical Flow Law)

Darcy’s method is a laboratory-based physical law describing fluid flow through porous media using core samples.

Darcy Equation:

k = (Q × μ × L) / (A × ΔP)

Parameter Relationships

  • Flow rate (Q): Directly proportional
  • Viscosity (μ): Directly proportional
  • Length (L): Directly proportional resistance
  • Area (A): Inversely proportional
  • Pressure drop (ΔP): Inversely proportional

Key Difference Summary

  • Empirical = log-based estimation
  • Darcy = laboratory measurement
  • Empirical = indirect prediction
  • Darcy = direct physical measurement
